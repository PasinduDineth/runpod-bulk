THE ADVENTURE OF THE NINTH HOUR
CHAPTER I: THE GENTLEMAN WHO KNEW THE HOUR
EDWARD WHITMORE — DIALOGUE

[Voice Description: Polished but visibly terrified. Whitmore addresses Holmes formally for the first time; begin with brittle composure, then deliver the prediction in a low, urgent voice, as if the words have become unbearable to keep inside.]
“Mr. Sherlock Holmes? My name is Edward Whitmore. At nine o'clock tonight I shall be murdered.”

[Voice Description: Controlled panic repeatedly breaking through social polish. Speak the sequence clearly, accelerating slightly through the two fulfilled predictions, then slow and tighten on the threat of nine.]
“That is what I cannot tell you. I have received three letters. The first foretold the destruction of a portrait in my study. The second foretold that a man I had not seen for eighteen years would speak to me outside my bank. Both occurred, to the minute. The third says I shall die at nine.”

[Voice Description: Refined, proud, and increasingly vulnerable. Whitmore asks for help from men he has just met; let fear thin his composure, but keep the plea sincere and restrained.]
“My wife believes I should leave London. My secretary has begged me to do the same. But I know the letters will follow me. I have come to you because I do not want to die.”

[Voice Description: Stiff and defensive, trying to minimize the matter too quickly. Let nervous tension show under his composure, particularly around Robert Keene's name.]
“He was a business associate. A disagreeable one. Surely that is not important.”
